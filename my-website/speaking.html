🧭 Workshop facilitator

Seeds to Harvest: a commercialisation workshop

Phytochemical Society of North America annual meeting · Madison, Wisconsin · 2026

A 90-minute workshop I designed and facilitated at PSNA 2026, one of three run that year to get plant scientists thinking about how to translate their own ideas. It works through the questions that come first: is there a real problem, what is the solution, who is the market, and how would you find out. I invited a speaker who had built a company around plant natural products, and brought in colleagues from the university technology transfer office to work with the participants.

Editorial work

Continuing position

Research Highlights Editor, The Plant Journal

Wiley · 2026 to present · part time, ongoing

I select significant papers published in The Plant Journal and write the Research Highlights that introduce them to a broad readership. In practice it means reading closely outside my own field every month, interviewing the authors, and finding the argument that makes a specialist result matter to everyone else.

It is the largest of my editorial commitments and the one that has done most for how I read and explain other people's science.

Speaker kit

Short bio · 50 words

Dr Charlay Wood is a plant biochemist at the University of Wisconsin–Madison. She studies how plants build new chemistry and how those pathways can be redesigned, turning them towards pigments, pharmaceuticals and flavours. She is Research Highlights Editor at The Plant Journal, and works on the commercialisation of plant science.

Medium bio · 100 words

Dr Charlay Wood is a plant biochemist at the University of Wisconsin–Madison, working between metabolic engineering and synthetic biology to redirect plant metabolism towards high-value compounds. Her PhD at the University of York identified OLADO, a bacterial-like decarboxy-oxidase that runs the first committed step of a plant alkaloid pathway, published in New Phytologist in 2026. Her entrepreneurial background runs from her own business in 2016 through direct sales and start-up operations to Natural Red Pigments, an exploration of whether engineered soybean could supply a natural red colourant, where she led the customer discovery as NSF I-Corps Entrepreneurial Lead. She holds a continuing part-time editorial position as Research Highlights Editor at The Plant Journal, is a 2026 Plantae Fellow, and has won three Best Speaker Awards.

What I speak about

Plant metabolic engineering and synthetic biology; enzyme discovery and parallel evolution; taking a plant-derived compound from discovery to product; and the commercialisation of academic science, as a talk or as a workshop. Length and level adjust to the room.

July 2022 · York, UK · Competition final

Three Minute Thesis: “Alkaloids: For Killing, Curing and Cooking”

The Three Minute Thesis challenge asks PhD researchers to explain their work to a non-specialist audience in three minutes, with a single static slide. I was a finalist in the University of York's 2022 competition, presenting on how understanding plant alkaloid biosynthesis could make valuable drugs like morphine cheaper and less wasteful to produce.

A brilliant exercise in distilling years of research into something anyone can connect with.
